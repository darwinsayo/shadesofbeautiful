Sometimes I just look up.

If there's a good sunset, I'll pull over. If a storm is coming, I'll watch it. At night, if the sky is dark enough, I'll go find something to put under the stars, like an old barn.

Nobody pays me for these. I do them because it keeps me practicing. Light is light. A sunset teaches me the same things a portrait does, only the sky doesn't mind if I take a long time.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/sky-sunset.jpg" alt="A farmhouse under a pink and orange sunset sky" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>The best color is often after the sun is gone, so I wait longer than I want to.</figcaption>

I've figured out a few things. Night sky pictures need a tripod and some patience. The exposure is long, so any shake ruins it.

For storms, I stay far away and out of the rain. Lightning is not worth getting hurt over. Really.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/sky-lightning.jpg" alt="Lightning over a flooded field under a purple storm sky" loading="lazy">
  <figcaption>A storm over a flooded field.</figcaption>
</figure>

That's all stuff I'd shoot anyway. It's nice to have somewhere to put it.
