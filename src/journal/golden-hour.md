Most of my portraits happen late in the day. Not always, but when I can choose, I choose that.

The light is low and warm. It comes in from the side, so faces get shape instead of flat shadows. Skin looks better. Hair picks up a glow. You can see it in the photo at the top, where the light is coming from behind her and lighting up the edge of her hair.

It also changes every few minutes. That's the hard part. I can't stand someone in one spot and take forty pictures. By the time I'm done the light is gone. So I move fast and keep talking, so nobody feels like they're being studied.

Where we stand matters too. I like to walk around a place before we start and figure out where the sun is going to be.

If it's cloudy, we still go. Clouds work like a big soft window. I just don't promise a sunset.

So if you're picking a time for a portrait session, ask for the last hour or so before sunset. We'll plan around where the sun will be.
